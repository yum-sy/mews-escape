# Gameplay presentation update

The Phaser runtime now gives the 15 offensive rewards and baseline Confusion separate animated projectile silhouettes, impact particles and synthesized attack/impact sounds. The most recently acquired offensive move owns the silhouette; support upgrades preserve it. Earlier homing, burn, freeze, poison, chain, drain, split and ricochet effects remain cumulative. Rank adds visible accents. Chain hits draw jagged links and drain hits send green motes toward Mew. Reward cards preview the resulting primary silhouette and the HUD names its rank.

## Attack mechanics

The latest offensive reward now also selects the attack mechanic. Flamethrower is a held, expanding short flame cone. Ice Beam is a held long ray; Water Gun is a medium-range continuous stream; Giga Drain is a short, gently tracking tether. Psybeam fires spaced pulses, while Thunder Shock and Thunderbolt discharge instantly (short/long reach). Icy Wind spreads three shards, Ember spreads two embers, Poison Sting travels faster, and Dragon Pulse/Sludge Bomb travel more slowly with larger collision bodies. Swift, Shadow Ball and Tri Attack retain homing, piercing and multishot roles.

Channels trace the same swept segments that are rendered. Furniture blocks them; room walls reflect them only with ricochet. Homing bends the actual path. Tri Attack makes multiple paths, penetration limits contacts, and later contacts retain 80% direct power. The flame cone has one additional contact. Burn, slow, poison, critical hits, knockback and direct-hit healing share the projectile hit routine. Chain/blast/split trigger on the first contact of each damage tick, and split fragments cannot recursively trigger these effects or healing. Prior effects remain cumulative even when another technique takes over the primary mechanic.

Continuous ticks and pulse attacks scale direct damage and knockback by their interval relative to the original attack interval. Fan pellets divide the volley damage instead of multiplying it. This preserves nominal single-target direct DPS; the intentional gameplay changes are reach, coverage and instant versus travelling hits. Aiming releases channels immediately. Pause, room transition and game end clear them. Checkpoint move IDs reconstruct the mechanic without a save migration.

Music uses a Web Audio lookahead sequencer with separate music/effect buses, envelopes, stereo voices and a master compressor. B6–B1 combat tempos are 64/74/84/96/108/116 BPM with increasingly dense accompaniment; cleared rooms have slower, sparse arrangements without drums. Mewtwo uses 112/138/166 BPM for confrontation, jealousy and abandonment, with denser bass, melody and percussion across phases. Room entry, resume and boss transitions select the arrangement; pause/menu mute active sources, and hidden tabs do not build a backlog. The existing game sound button enables audio through a user gesture. Opening audio remains independent.
